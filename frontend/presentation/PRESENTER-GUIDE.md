## Jury Q&A

**Are these real measurements?**

No. Every watt in the demo comes from a deterministic energy model with 17 appliance profiles, and the app labels it “Données simulées”. We did not modify a 230 V adapter. Readings from real hardware would arrive through POST /api/mesures, which exists, validates and deduplicates readings and accepts batches; we tested it with a headless simulator, not with real hardware.

**Is this AI? Is there machine learning?**

It is rule-based on purpose. We have little data, and every alert must explain itself. Five rules: overconsumption, standby, simultaneous heavy loads, peak hours, monthly goal. A learned model is on the roadmap, to be compared against these rules on real data.

**How do you avoid false alarms?**

A single spike never triggers an alert: it takes three consecutive readings. The usual level is a median over three days, which outliers barely move, and readings already flagged as anomalous are excluded. The gap must also exceed 10 W or 8 % of the usual level. After a correction, the rule stays quiet for two minutes. We have not measured the false-alarm rate on real homes yet; the pilot is how we would.

**What does “confidence 88 %” mean?**

It is a heuristic that grows with the size of the gap: 70 % at the threshold, capped at 97 %. It is not a calibrated probability. Calibrating it needs labelled real data.

**What does “Appliquer la correction” actually do?**

It records the action and the appliance’s reference power, marks the appliance optimised, and starts measuring savings against that reference. In the simulation the corrected setting is applied as an efficiency factor; in a real home the person changes the setting. Mieruka never switches anything off.

**How are tokens calculated? Can people game them?**

500 tokens per kWh avoided against the appliance’s own reference, rounded down, at most 400 a day. Only verified savings count. Whether that resists gaming in real homes is part of what a pilot has to show.

**What does STEG get, and what about privacy?**

A building view: the collective meter split into monitored appliances and the untracked rest, incidents, peak load. Households appear only as pseudonyms (FY-xxxx), and regions with fewer than ten households are masked. A test checks that no names leak. This is our proposal; STEG is not a partner.

**Are STEG, JICA or ESPRIT partners?**

No. The event and partner logos on our landing page are there because we built this at KIZUNA 2026; they do not imply endorsement. We only use public data: the STEG residential tariff in force since 1 May 2022, STEG’s 2019 household survey and an IEA emission factor.

**How accurate is the bill estimate?**

It applies the public STEG residential low-voltage tariff (since 1 May 2022) to simulated consumption, including an estimate for appliances without an adapter. It is labelled an estimate in the app.

**What would real hardware need?**

A certified measuring plug or meter that can send an adapter ID, a timestamp and watts to POST /api/mesures, one reading or a batch. An optional adapter key protects the endpoint, and an invalid batch is rejected as a whole.

**Does it scale?**

Not proven. The prototype runs on Django with SQLite locally, and the repository has configuration for a hosted libSQL (Turso) database. We have not load-tested it. That is part of preparing for deployment.

**Why would households use it?**

Our hypothesis: specific, appliance-level advice with a cost attached, plus rewards for verified savings. We have not tested it with real households yet; that is the first step of the roadmap.

**Where is the Tunisia–Japan link?**

The idea comes from Japanese industry: make a problem visible before you fix it. The app also compares a Tunisian household (1,636 kWh a year, STEG survey 2019) with a Japanese one (3,911 kWh, Japan’s Ministry of the Environment, fiscal 2023), with sources on screen.

## Before going on stage

1. Run Django on the presenting laptop (uv run manage.py runserver). Do not depend on venue Wi-Fi.
2. Open http://127.0.0.1:8000/login in a browser and sign in with the household demo account (Amira).
3. In « Démo guidée », reset the demo (↺) so the alert list is clean, then mark steps 1–2 done (« Passer cette étape ») so step 3, the AC fault, is one click away.
4. Set the browser zoom to 125 %, close other tabs and silence notifications.
5. Open mieruka-pitch.html in a second window, press F for full screen and P for the presenter window (on the laptop screen when the projector extends the display).
6. Rehearse switching between the deck and the app (Alt+Tab) until it takes under two seconds.
7. After each rehearsal, reset the demo again: an applied correction closes the AC alert.
8. If the live demo fails, press B on the demo slide: seven real screenshots from the running prototype.
